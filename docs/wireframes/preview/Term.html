Endorsement

Also known as: policy change, rider (life & health), mid-term adjustment

Medium usageBeginnerAll insurance typesPolicy wordingAI-generated · AI-reviewed
Check yourself
Quick answer

An endorsement is an official change to an insurance policy after it starts, such as a new address, an added driver or extra coverage. It becomes part of the policy and can raise or lower the premium.

In plain English

Your policy is a contract. When something in your life changes, you don’t buy a new policy. The insurer adds an endorsement, a written update that changes part of the contract.

Example

Adding your teenage son as a driver on your car policy is done with an endorsement.

Tom’s story

Quote & Bind

Tom buys auto insurance for $100/month with $50,000 liability coverage.

2
Issue

He gets his policy documents on the 1st.

3
Changes

On the 20th, Tom moves to a new city and wants to add collision coverage. His insurer makes both changes with an endorsement.

4
Result

The new address and added coverage raise his premium to $120/month, starting the 20th.

5
Renewal

At renewal, the policy continues with the new address and coverage included.
